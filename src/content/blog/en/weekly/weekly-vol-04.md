## First, the Binding Result

Last issue I tied those twenty minutes to “right after washing up”. This week: six days.

The change is tangible — I no longer make a decision before I sit down. The thing has stopped requiring the step of talking myself into it; like brushing my teeth, the action comes first and the reason afterwards, or no reason at all.

Stability brought something else with it, though: it stopped giving me a sense of achievement. On the sixth day I felt no pleasure, only a flat “well, that is how it goes”. And right behind that came a very familiar impulse: since this is so stable, surely I can add one more thing.

I held it off this week. Looking back over these four issues, every problem came not from doing too little but from wanting to add too much.

## Three Small Things This Week

**One: I wrote down a minimum version — one page still counts.** On Thursday I got back very late and had planned to skip. With the rule changed to “one page still counts”, I sat down for one page — and read four. Allowing the smallest version holds up far better than demanding all or nothing.

**Two: I noticed that reading smoothly is when nothing sticks.** Two days this week ran fast and fluent, and afterwards I could not recall a single sentence. One day ran slow, stuck on a paragraph I read three times, and that sentence is still with me. **Fluency and retention are different things.**

**Three: I wrote the urge to add a thing down, then did not act on it.** Once written, the impulse became something I could check against; two days later most of it had gone. An impulse needs an outlet, not fulfilment.

## One Thought

I used to treat enthusiasm as the fuel of persistence, so a drop in enthusiasm looked like the beginning of the end.

Now I split it differently: **enthusiasm starts things, routine keeps them.** Anything launched on enthusiasm alone rarely survives three weeks; what survives has turned into routine — unglamorous, but present every day.

> The most tempting mistake after stabilising is to add more while the fire is hot; and once you do, the first thing loosens too.

So this week I am holding off on the second thing.

## Only One Thing for Next Week

No second thing. One rule change only: write “one page still counts” into the rules formally, somewhere I can see it.

No further weekly accounting for this one: it has become part of the day, like sleeping or brushing teeth — nothing that needs supervising.
