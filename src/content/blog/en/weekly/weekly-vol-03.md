## First, the Twenty-Minute Result

Last issue I cut that half hour down to twenty minutes. This week: five days, two more than the week before.

The extra two days did not come from trying harder. The real difference is that the number is not intimidating — before sitting down I no longer have to talk myself into it, or first weigh whether today is worth it.

One more concrete finding: what makes me quit is almost never a lack of time. It is **the phone sitting within reach**. It needs no reason; it only needs one absent-minded reach.

## Three Small Things This Week

**One: moved the phone charger into the living room.** One action, and the whole evening’s back-and-forth tug disappeared. Changing the environment clearly costs less than changing the will.

**Two: wrote down something trivial but useful.** On all five successful days I sat down at the same moment — right after washing up. On the two days when the time floated, nothing happened. Which suggests **when** matters more than **how long**.

**Three: squeezed the distance between “intending to do it” and “doing it” down to a single step.** Anything that requires “getting ready first” mostly never gets done. Put the place, the tool and the doorway in position beforehand, and starting is down to one motion.

## One Thought

I used to explain every failure as a shortage of willpower, so I set harsher targets, failed harder, and grew more certain that my willpower was the problem. An efficient loop, except that nothing changed.

Now I would rather treat it as a physics problem: move the obstacle, nail down the trigger, and only then does willpower get its turn — and it only has to push a little.

> Do not ask yourself why you cannot keep it up. First ask how much resolve this thing needs before it can begin.

The more resolve it demands, the more likely the design is wrong.

## Only One Thing for Next Week

Bind those twenty minutes to “right after washing up”.

No more “before sleep” — that is a stretch of time without edges, and it can be pushed back indefinitely, all the way into tomorrow. Result next issue.
