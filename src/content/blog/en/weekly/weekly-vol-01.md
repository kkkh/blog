## Record First, Judge Later

This column is new: **Weekly Notes**. Once a week I sit down and fold the past week — what got done, what got read, what would not leave me alone — into a single page. No chasing news, no padding for length. On a week with nothing to say, three lines count as filing on time.

I carved it out because I kept noticing that “worth remembering” and “worth a full article” are two different things. Most of what deserves remembering cannot fill an article on its own; but piled together, that is the actual shape of a year.

## Three Small Things This Week

**One: keep a stretch of the morning for myself first.** No rush to the phone — a glass of water first, then the thing I least want to do goes at the very top of the day. A few days of this taught me that the hard part is rarely the task; it is the act of starting.

**Two: read a book slowly.** Twenty pages a day, copying out any sentence I liked. Slowing down revealed that the same paragraph gives far more than it does when skimmed.

**Three: count how often attention gets cut in half.** The result stung a little: a single stretch of focus rarely lasts past twenty minutes. But writing the number down made it less nerve-racking — once a problem is seen, it turns into something you can work on.

## One Thought

I used to think of a record as a private ledger with no real value. More and more it looks like **leaving a path for a later version of myself**: when the day comes that I need to confirm what actually happened during a stretch of time, there is at least one page I can check.

> Keeping a record is not about how much a given day mattered. It is about being able to confirm, many days later, that you really did walk through it.

## Only One Thing for Next Week

Give the half hour before sleep to a paper book, and leave the phone in another room. Just that one. Nothing more.
