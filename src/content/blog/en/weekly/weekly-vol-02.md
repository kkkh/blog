## First, the Result of Last Issue’s One Thing

At the end of the last issue I set myself a single task: give the half hour before sleep to a paper book, and leave the phone in another room.

The result is not flattering — three days out of seven. On two nights the phone made its way back to the bedside table; on two others I turned a couple of pages and fell asleep.

By my old standard that counts as failure, and the whole thing gets filed under “things I cannot do”. This time I changed the arithmetic: **three days is not a failure, it is three days.** What matters is whether I can pick it up again on day four after a break — and this week I did, so it never went back to zero.

## Three Small Things This Week

**One: write the full list of things I want to do, then force myself to strike three out.** Fill the list as usual, then delete three — not postpone, delete. Looking back, not one of the three crossed-out items came back to mind. What I genuinely want has always been less than what I write down.

**Two: reread a note from six months ago.** The same passage: half a year back I took from it “what to remember”; this time I took “what I can leave unremembered”. I had not changed; the question had, and the same page shifted a layer of meaning.

**Three: changed the quilt on the first cold day.** A small thing, and it quietened the whole evening. Some troubles do not need solving — they need handling one step early, and the rest of the annoyance never arrives.

## One Thought

I used to read “persistence” as never missing a day, which means one miss voids the whole thing.

That bar is too steep. A more workable version: **persistence is not never breaking, it is being able to resume after breaking.** Doing it daily leans on willpower; coming back after a gap leans on treating it as an ordinary part of the day rather than a test.

> Whether a thing is worth doing shows up in whether you are still doing it three years from now — not in whether this week scored full marks.

## Only One Thing for Next Week

Cut that half hour down to twenty minutes.

Not surrender — a lower threshold. Twenty minutes is easier to actually do, and only what gets done produces a day four.
